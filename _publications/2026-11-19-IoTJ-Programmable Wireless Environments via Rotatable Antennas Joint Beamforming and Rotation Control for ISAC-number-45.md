Rotatable antennas (RAs) offer a practical and low-complexity solution for realizing programmable wireless environments by dynamically reconfiguring the propagation medium. 
In this paper, we investigate an RA-enabled ISAC system that serves multiple users and senses multiple targets simultaneously. Specifically, we formulate two optimization problems for this scenario, namely, the communication-centric and sensing-centric problems. To tackle these problems, we propose two alternating optimization (AO)-based algorithms to jointly optimize the transmit beamforming at the base station and the antenna rotation angle. For the communication-centric problem, fractional programming (FP) is adopted for beamforming optimization, while a successive convex approximation (SCA)-based algorithm is developed for rotation angle optimization. For the sensing-centric problem, semidefinite relaxation (SDR) is employed for beamforming, and an SCA-penalty-based algorithm is devised for rotation angle optimization. Finally, we provide simulation results to evaluate the performance of the proposed solutions for both problems. These results demonstrate that RAs effectively enhance the ISAC performance, confirming RAs as a promising enabler of smart radio environments for next-generation ISAC systems.
